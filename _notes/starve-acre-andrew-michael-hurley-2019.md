[[BookNotes]] author [[Andrew Michael Hurley]]

Folk horror novella about a grieving couple in the country. 

- Sets a trap and draws it tight so slowly, until you are caught without realising. Similar in this way to *[[The Wicker Man]]*.
- Has a lot of comparison to [[Lanny-Max-Porter]]- elder pagan god stealing a child, but no relief. Only see it from their perspective, not the Gods.
- Sets up points of horror- the seance, the village, but things happen in a strange, unexpected and unnerving way.
- Horrible, uncanny images - the hare knitting itself together, a grieving woman suddenly painting the nursery white.
- Tense and unsettling throughout. Ends on a sudden image of horror with no aftermath - like [[Barrowbeck-by-Andrew-Michael-Hurley-2024]], there is no epilogue, only the sudden, inevitable end of the story.
